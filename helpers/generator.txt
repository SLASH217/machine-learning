
### Why Generators are Slower (The "Context Switch" Tax)

In our previous discussion about how CPython implements `yield`, we talked about how it bundles your function's variables into a "Frame Object" on the heap.

When you use a List Comprehension, Python runs a highly optimized C-loop (`LIST_APPEND`) that just blasts through the math as fast as the CPU allows.

When you use a Generator, Python has to do heavy administrative work for **every single item**:

1. It pauses the function.
2. It packages the state into the Frame Object.
3. It hands you the value.
4. When you ask for the next value, it unpacks the Frame Object.
5. It figures out which line of code it left off on.
6. It resumes the math.

If your data easily fits into your RAM, a list comprehension will beat a generator in raw speed every single time because it avoids all of that pausing and unpacking overhead.

### The 3 Major Cons of Generators

Aside from the slight speed penalty per item, saving all that RAM comes with strict functional limitations.

#### 1. They are "One-Time Use" (Exhaustion)

A list is permanent. You can loop over a list, print it, and then loop over it again.
A generator is a one-way street. Once it yields a value, it forgets it forever. Once the generator finishes its final calculation, it is physically empty.

* If you try to loop over a generator a second time, Python will just do nothing. It won't throw an error; it will just act like the generator is an empty list. To use it again, you have to completely recreate the generator variable from scratch.

#### 2. No Indexing or Slicing

Because the data doesn't physically exist in memory yet, you cannot jump around.

* You **can** do `my_list[500]` to instantly grab the 500th item.
* You **cannot** do `my_generator[500]`. If you want the 500th item from a generator, you have no choice but to manually call `next()` 500 times, forcing it to calculate and throw away the first 499 items.

#### 3. You Cannot Use `len()`

If you need to know how many items are in your dataset before you process it, a generator is useless.

* Because it evaluates lazily, the generator itself has absolutely no idea how many items it will eventually produce until it hits the very end. If you type `len(my_generator)`, Python will throw a `TypeError`.

### The Golden Rule of Choosing

* **If you need speed, need to jump around the data, or need to loop over it multiple times:** Use a List Comprehension (assuming it fits in your RAM).
* **If the data is massive, or you only need to read it exactly once from top to bottom:** Use a Generator.
